Welcome to FlexRecorder!

FlexRecorder is a simple, fully accessible audio recorder. Capture sound from your microphone, listen back to it, shape it with volume, pitch, pan, and various other effects, and save the result as an MP3 file. Everything is spoken aloud and driven from easy button and list menus, so you can use the whole app with your eyes closed.

Getting started

When you open FlexRecorder, you'll land on the main menu, which has two buttons:

Start recording: begins capturing audio from your chosen microphone right away.
Preferences: lets you choose which audio devices the app uses for playback and recording.

To make a recording, press Start recording. While you're capturing, three buttons appear:

Stop recording: finishes the recording and takes you straight into the audio player so you can hear what you recorded.
Pause recording: pauses capture without ending the recording. The button changes to Resume recording, and pressing it again picks up right where you left off, adding onto the same take. You can pause and resume as many times as you like.

Cancel recording: throws the recording away and returns you to the main menu.

You can't leave the app while a recording is in progress, whether it's running or paused. Stop or cancel the recording first, then press escape on the main menu to exit.

The audio player

Once you've made a recording, the audio player opens automatically. It's organized into three distinct tabs that you can move between with your arrow keys.

Player tab

Start playback: starts playback. While it's playing, the button reads Stop playback, so the same button stops it again. Playback also stops on its own when the recording reaches the end.
Pause playback: pauses playback and keeps your place, so you can pick up right where you left off. The button then reads Resume playback, and pressing it again continues from that exact spot. This button only appears while something is playing or paused; a full stop always starts over from the beginning.

Save recording: writes your recording to a file (see "Saving" below).
Clear recording: throws the current recording away and returns you to the main menu.

Controls tab

Three sliders that change how your recording sounds, and you'll hear the change as you adjust them:

Volume: how loud the recording plays. Slide it down to make the sound quieter, or all the way up for full volume.
Pitch: how high or low the recording sounds. Lower values make it deeper and slower; higher values make it higher and faster, like speeding the recording up.
Pan: where the sound sits between your left and right ears. Center keeps it balanced in both ears, while sliding left or right moves it toward that side.

After the pan slider you'll find three reset buttons: Reset volume, Reset pitch, and Reset pan. Each one snaps its slider back to its default in a single press. If the slider is already at its default, the button tells you so instead of doing nothing.

Effects tab

An effect list where you can pick one effect to apply: echo, reverb, lowpass, highpass, chorus, distortion, phaser, or flanger. Choose "none" to turn effects off. When you pick an effect, a set of sliders appears for finetuning it, and your changes are applied as you listen. Only one effect can be active at a time. Each time you switch to a different effect, its sliders start fresh at their default values.
Quick tip: from the controls tab or the effects tab you can press enter while on a slider or on the effect list to start and stop playback without leaving the tab.

Leaving the player

Pressing escape in the audio player discards the recording and returns you to the main menu. If you want to keep your work, save it first.

Saving

When you save, FlexRecorder writes an MP3 file named after the current date and time, so your saves never overwrite each other. Everything you set in the player is included in the saved file, so the volume, pitch, pan, and effect you hear during playback are exactly what you get in the MP3. After saving, your recording stays open in the player so you can keep listening, adjust it further, or save another version.

Preferences

The preferences screen lets you configure the following:

Audio channel: whether recordings are captured in mono (one channel) or stereo (two channels). Mono makes smaller files; stereo is the default.
Sample rate: how detailed the recording is, from 11.025 kHz up to 48 kHz. Higher rates capture more of the sound's high end and sound clearer, while lower rates make smaller files. 44.1 kHz (CD quality) is the default.

Conversion quality: the bitrate, from 32 kbps up to 320 kbps, used when your recording is saved to an MP3. Higher values sound closer to the original and make larger files; lower values make smaller files. 192 kbps is the default.

Playback device: which output (speakers or headphones) your recordings play through.
Recording device: which microphone the app records from.

The two device lists show your system default device first, followed by every other device in alphabetical order. Press Okay to apply and remember your choices, or Cancel to leave them unchanged. All of these settings are saved and restored automatically the next time you open the app.

Effect reference

Here's what each effect on the effects tab does, along with the sliders it gives you. Remember, only one effect can be active at a time, and switching effects always starts its sliders back at their defaults.

Echo

Repeats the sound after a short delay, like a shout bouncing back in a canyon, a mountain range, or an empty warehouse.

dry mix: the volume of the original, untouched sound. Turning it down pushes the sound off into the distance.
wet mix: the volume of the echoed repeats layered on top.

feedback: how many times the echo repeats. Low values give a single clean bounce; high values give many repeats that slowly fade away.
delay: how long before the first echo is heard. Short delays feel like a small room; long delays feel like a wide open outdoor space.

Reverb

Blends many tiny reflections into a smooth tail to recreate the natural ambience of a room. Unlike echo there are no separate repeats, just a sense of the space itself. This is the most natural-sounding effect for indoor places.

dry mix: the volume of the original, untouched sound.
wet mix: the volume of the reverb tail mixed in. This one goes higher than on other effects so the reverb can fully wash over the sound.

room size: how big the space feels, from a small closet up to a cathedral or cavern.
damping: how soft the walls are. Low values give a bright, reflective space like a tiled room; high values give a soft, muffled space like a carpeted room or a forest.

width: how far the reverb spreads between your ears. Low keeps it centered; high spreads it wide.

Lowpass

Removes the high frequencies and lets the low ones through, giving a muffled, dark sound, as if it's distant, blocked, or underwater.

frequency: the cutoff point. Sounds above it are reduced, so lower values muffle more heavily, like listening through a thick wall.
bandwidth: how gradually the cutoff kicks in, from a gentle fade to a sharper edge.
quality: adds a slight emphasis right at the cutoff. Low values stay flat and natural; higher values add a touch of color.

Highpass

The opposite of lowpass: it removes the low frequencies and lets the high ones through, giving a thin, hollow, telephone-like sound.

frequency: the cutoff point. Sounds below it are reduced, so higher values thin the sound out more, stripping away the bass.
bandwidth: how gradually the cutoff kicks in, from a gentle fade to a sharper edge.
quality: adds a slight emphasis right at the cutoff. Low values stay flat and natural; higher values add a touch of color.

Chorus

Layers several slightly detuned copies of the sound together for a thick, shimmering, wide quality, the kind you often hear on guitars and vocals in music. Great for a magical, underwater, or dreamlike feel.

dry mix: the volume of the original, untouched sound.
wet mix: the volume of the chorused layers mixed in.

feedback: how strong the effect is. Low values give a subtle shimmer; high values give a denser, swirling sound.

min sweep: the shortest point of the wobble.
max sweep: the longest point of the wobble. A bigger gap between min and max gives a wider, more dramatic sweep.

rate: how fast the wobble moves. Lower values gently undulate; higher values wobble faster and more noticeably.

Distortion

Roughens and saturates the sound for a gritty, aggressive texture. Well suited to anything that should feel harsh, broken, or electrical.

drive: how hard the sound is pushed. Low values add mild warmth; high values give heavy, grinding distortion.

dry mix: the volume of the original, untouched sound.
wet mix: the volume of the distorted sound mixed in.

feedback: feeds the distortion back into itself for a more extreme, unstable sound.
volume: the overall level after distortion. Since distortion tends to get louder, use this to bring the level back down.

Phaser

Sweeps a filter across the sound and mixes it back with the original, producing a swirling, jet-like whoosh. Good for a supernatural, dizzying, or otherworldly feel.

dry mix: the volume of the original, untouched sound.
wet mix: the volume of the swept sound mixed in.

rate: how fast the swirl moves, from a slow creep to a rapid flutter.
depth: how wide the swirl is, from subtle to dramatic.

feedback: sharpens the effect, making it more metallic and resonant.
phase: where in the sweep the effect starts. The default works well for most uses.

Flanger

Similar to the phaser but with a more hollow, metallic, jet-engine-like sweep, famous from classic science-fiction sound design and electric guitars. For the classic flanger sound, keep both delay sliders low.

dry mix: the volume of the original, untouched sound.
wet mix: the volume of the flanged sound mixed in.

feedback: makes the sweep more hollow and metallic; high values give the most extreme character.

min delay: the shortest point of the sweep. Keep it low for a true flanger sound.
max delay: the longest point of the sweep. Keep it low too; a bigger gap between min and max gives a wider sweep.

rate: how fast the sweep moves, from a slow jet-like glide to a faster mechanical wobble.

Enjoy, and happy recording!
